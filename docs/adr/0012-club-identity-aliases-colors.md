# 0012 — Club identity, aliases and colors

Status: **Accepted** (2026-10-16). Written with plan 0007 (issue #39). The product decisions on the open points are recorded in the plan under "Decisions recorded".

## Context

`data/clubs.json` is the first real data file. Every later file (matches, titles, rivalries, coverage) references clubs by `id`, and every ingestion adapter (openfootball, Kaggle, API-Football, RSSSF) has to turn a source's own spelling of a team ("Vélez Sarsfield", "Velez", "Central Cba (SdE)") into that `id`. The generic crest (ADR 0005) and the future club theming (ADR 0007, "Club theming will need to re-validate contrast") both read `colors` and `jersey_pattern`. The schema (ADR 0009, `src/data-model/schemas.ts`) fixes the shape but not the meaning of these fields: what an id looks like and whether it may change, how a source name is matched, which shirt color is "primary", and where hex values come from.

## Decision

1. **Ids are stable slugs.** Lowercase ASCII, words joined by `-`, diacritics removed, matching `^[a-z0-9]+(-[a-z0-9]+)*$`. The id is derived once from the club's common name; a city qualifier is added only when a source itself disambiguates the club with a city code (the Kaggle file writes `Estudiantes (LP)` because it also has `Estudiantes (BA)`, so the id is `estudiantes-la-plata`); clubs the sources name bare keep the bare name. **An id never changes** after it is merged, even if the club changes its name; renames only touch `name`, `short_name` and `aliases`.
2. **Aliases are the only bridge from source names to ids.** `aliases` holds every exact spelling used by every ingested source (openfootball, Kaggle, later API-Football and RSSSF), even when it equals `name`. Adapters resolve a source name by **exact match** after Unicode NFC normalization and trimming; no prefix, fuzzy or case-insensitive matching at ingestion time (the Kaggle file has `Huracan` and `Huracan (C)`, `Racing Club` and `Racing (C)`, `Independiente` and `Ind Rivadavia`). An unresolved name stops the ingestion with an error; it is never guessed.
3. **No alias is shared by two clubs**, compared after folding (NFC, lower case, diacritics removed, whitespace collapsed), so a future tolerant resolver can never be ambiguous. Aliases are global, not per source; the residual risk (a bare `Estudiantes` meaning Estudiantes de La Plata in openfootball) is accepted and checked by the adapters: within one season, two different source names must not resolve to the same id.
4. **Color semantics.** `primary` is the body color of the home shirt (for equal-share patterns, stripes and halves, the first color in the club's official color description, as written in the cited article). `secondary` is the pattern color: the second stripe color, the sash, the hoops or band, the other half, or, for `solid`, the main trim color. `tertiary` is only used when the official colors name a third color. Official colors are facts taken from the cited source; **hex values are the project's own choice**, picked from a small named palette in code and tuned only within the same color family to meet contrast.
5. **Contrast is part of the data contract.** Every club's `primary` versus `secondary` reaches at least 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11, non-text, `meetsAA(ratio, "ui")` in `src/lib/contrast.ts`), so the pattern of a generic crest is visible. Every club has at least one of its colors at 3:1 or more against `--background` and `--background-raised`, so club theming (ADR 0007) can pick an accent without inventing a color. If a club's real colors cannot meet these rules at any faithful shade, the record is not forced: Diego decides.
6. **No brand material.** No crest, logo, photo or brand manual is stored or linked as a color source (ADR 0005).

## Consequences

- Adapters share one resolver (`resolveClubId`) and a failing resolution is a loud error, so a misspelt or new club cannot enter `matches.json` silently. New sources add aliases through a normal data PR.
- URLs and Supabase rows can use ids as permanent keys; renames are cheap.
- Tests enforce the slug format, alias uniqueness, the source-name fixtures and the contrast rules in CI, without the Kaggle CSV.
- A few real color pairs (for example blue and red) need a darker or lighter shade of one color to reach 3:1; the shade is a project choice and never claimed as the official hex.
- `jersey_pattern` has no value for a single horizontal band (one stripe across the chest). Decision: such shirts (for example Boca Juniors) are recorded as `other` for now, without changing the schema; issue #49 adds a `band` value, and the records are then updated.
- `short_name` is unique and has at most 15 characters (so `Central Córdoba` fits).
- `founded` is the **official** foundation year per the constitutive act, not the popular birth date. Example: Independiente is 1905 (constitutive assembly of 1 January 1905, es.wikipedia infobox "oficial" and Wikidata) although the club celebrates its birth on 4 August 1904; the popular date is documented in the verification log of plan 0007.
- A pattern that cannot be verified is recorded as `solid` (safest reading): Defensa y Justicia (stripe direction not stated by any source). Deportivo Riestra is `solid` because its current home shirt (since 2012) is black with white details; the stripes are its traditional kit.
- The more intense celeste (`celeste intenso`, `#3b94e0`) is accepted for Racing, Atlético Tucumán and Belgrano (the base celeste does not reach 3:1 against white). Independiente Rivadavia was left out at first (no verifiable second color) and was added by plan 0012 with its home-kit trim as `secondary`, see ADR 0016. The `band` pattern value is issue #49 and the About-page credits (Wikipedia, openfootball) are issue #50.
- Decisions on the other open points: display names are the common names (source spellings kept as aliases); short names as in the plan; city is `Buenos Aires` for the City and the locality for Greater Buenos Aires; `primary` is the dominant color of the home shirt body and `secondary` the pattern or trim color, choosing the most common home-shirt reading when sources differ in order; renamed clubs keep the current common name and ids never change; a club whose faithful colors cannot meet the contrast rules is not forced, it is left out and escalated.
